# Theories and Modes of Truth

## Core Question

What do we mean when we call something true?

The question appears simple, but it can ask several different things:

1. **What kind of thing is capable of being true?**
2. **What makes or validates a claim as true?**
3. **How does reality appear from within a particular consciousness or relationship?**
4. **How can true statements nevertheless be used to create false understanding?**

These questions generate four complementary notes:

| Mode | Central question | Primary concern |
|---|---|---|
| [[ideation/philosophy/propositional-truth|Propositional truth]] | What claim is true or false? | Meaning, logic, truth conditions, correspondence |
| [[ideation/philosophy/pragmatic-utility-truth|Pragmatic or utility truth]] | What survives inquiry and works in experience? | Verification, consequences, action, warranted belief |
| [[ideation/philosophy/relational-truth-qualia-perception|Relational truth]] | How is reality disclosed to a situated perceiver? | Qualia, perception, emotion, perspective, intersubjectivity |
| [[ideation/philosophy/deceptive-truth|Deceptive truth]] | What belief is a technically true statement being used to produce? | Implication, omission, framing, manipulation |

The extended companion note, [[ideation/philosophy/additional-theories-and-modes-of-truth]], develops twenty further theories, modes of disclosure, and specialized domains of truth.

## A Necessary Taxonomic Caution

These are not four rival theories at the same logical level.

- **Propositional truth** identifies a major *truth-bearer*: the content expressed by a declarative claim.
- **Pragmatic truth** proposes a *method or criterion of validation*: truth shows itself through inquiry, prediction, and consequences.
- **Relational truth** identifies a *domain of disclosure*: some realities appear only through a relation among object, perceiver, body, context, and language.
- **Deceptive truth** identifies an *ethical and communicative use*: a speaker may utter true propositions while intentionally producing a false model in the hearer.

Flattening these distinctions creates false conflicts. A statement can be propositionally true, pragmatically reliable, relationally conditioned, and deceptively presented at the same time.

## A Four-Question Method

When evaluating a truth claim, ask:

### 1. What exactly is being asserted?

Translate the utterance into the clearest proposition possible. Identify ambiguous terms, suppressed premises, indexicals, and scope.

### 2. How is the claim tested?

Ask what evidence, inquiry, prediction, practice, or lived consequence could confirm or disconfirm it. Practical success is evidence, but immediate usefulness is not automatically truth.

### 3. For whom, under what conditions, and in what relationship does it appear?

Color, pain, emotion, social meaning, and sacred experience cannot be described adequately without naming the perceiver and conditions of disclosure.

### 4. What belief is the communication designed to induce?

A sentence can be literally accurate while its framing, omissions, or implications are designed to make another person believe something false.

## Relation to the 5×3 Framework

The [[value-framework|5×3 Value Framework]] locates Correspondence at Mental/Spatial because truth concerns the relation between a mental model and what is outside or independent of that model.

The four modes refine that placement:

- **Propositional truth** describes the mental model that is being compared with reality.
- **Pragmatic truth** tests that model through Mental/Agency over time.
- **Relational truth** shows that correspondence is often mediated by embodiment, perspective, and interpersonal context.
- **Deceptive truth** reveals the difference between appreciating truth and exploiting it, connecting Correspondence to [[value-exploitation-vs-appreciation]].

The framework should therefore distinguish:

> A true sentence, a justified belief, a faithful description of experience, and an honest act of communication.

These often coincide, but they are not identical.

## Classical Theories Still in View

The four-note cluster does not replace the standard theories of truth:

| Theory | Basic claim |
|---|---|
| **Correspondence** | A claim is true when it matches a fact or reality. |
| **Coherence** | A belief is true or justified insofar as it belongs to a maximally coherent web of beliefs. |
| **Pragmatism** | Truth is inseparable from the practices and consequences of inquiry. |
| **Deflationism** | Calling a proposition true adds little or nothing beyond asserting the proposition itself. |

These theories answer different parts of the larger question. Coherence and pragmatic testing can function as epistemic methods while correspondence remains the metaphysical aim. Deflationism challenges whether truth needs a substantial metaphysical explanation at all.

## Cross-Cultural Reading Rule

Religious and cultural traditions should not be forced into modern Western categories. When a tradition values right speech, reliable cognition, saving knowledge, contemplative insight, or rectification of names, this may be an **analogue**, application, or critique of a truth theory rather than an exact instance of it.

The relevant questions are:

- What does this tradition treat as a reliable disclosure of reality?
- How does it distinguish knowledge from illusion?
- What ethical obligations govern speech?
- Does truth culminate in correct description, successful action, transformed perception, right relationship, or liberation?
